The problem

Floor robots can scrub a restroom floor, but toilets, urinals, sinks and litter need arms that reach from floor to mirror, in rooms built for people.

This is the dual-arm prototype of LionsBot T1, a restroom-cleaning robot from Griffin Labs, LionsBot’s embodied-AI research arm: two arms on a tall lift, on a swerve-drive base, that clean from the floor to the mirror. The teleoperation and data-collection setup I built on it fed the team’s VLA training, the work that led to Griffin Alpha, and I now lead the hardware of its single-arm successor.

One robot for the whole restroom

Section titled “One robot for the whole restroom”
  • ⤢

    Urinal: the lift puts one arm at bowl height while the other stays clear.

  • ⤢

    Toilet bowl: both arms work low, close to the base, with a jet of ozonated water for stubborn stains.

  • ⤢

    Sink and countertop: the same arms work at counter height, misting ozonated water to disinfect the surfaces.

  • ⤢

    Litter: the grippers pick up tissues and other rubbish.

Its cleaning tools run on ozonated water instead of chemicals: a fine mist disinfects surfaces, and a jet shifts stubborn stains.
