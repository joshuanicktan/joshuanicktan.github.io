Part of Organising RoboClash 2023

From plan to match day

I competed in RoboClash 2022, and its participants’ feedback asked for three things: more variety in the game, more guidance and support, and a better starter kit. RoboClash 2023 was planned around those three, from the first preparations in May 2022 to the competition on 10 to 12 January 2023.

The plan

Section titled “The plan”

The game started as a pitch to sponsors: a 3.5 m square playfield themed on recycling, with tasks at three levels of difficulty. The easiest was pushing a bin into the centre zone; the middle one, putting trash into a bin; the hardest, sorting coloured recyclables from trash and touching an 80 cm charging bar at the end. From September 2022, a showcase booth outside the canteen put past robots and game elements in front of students, to draw them in before registration opened. Then we marked out the playfield on the floor of the room where it would be built.

Building the playfield

Section titled “Building the playfield”

We laid the floor inside the taped square, built the walls and the centre tower that carries the charging bars and the ball dispenser, and tried the dispenser out while the playfield was still being built. In the final rules, every match’s balls drop from 1 m above the centre zone and scatter, so no two matches start the same way.

Getting every team building

Section titled “Getting every team building”

About half the participants were freshmen, so the support was planned as carefully as the game. The plan: registration from late October to mid-November 2022, then a game brief where teams collected their starter kits and radios and saw the StarterBot they could build from them (the StarterBot guide). I organised a series of workshops on 3D modelling, electronics, fabrication, Arduino and programming, and in the weeks before the competition teams tested on the playfield and came to open consults for help.

Match day

Section titled “Match day”
