Click&Go smart bike lock

A smart bike lock that locks when you push the bike onto a pole: 4 seconds, no bending down.

Role
Mechanical design, fabrication and FEA
Period
Jan 2023 to Apr 2023
Status
Complete
Context
School
Disciplines
Mechanical, Electrical, Firmware, Software, Manufacturing
Tools
Fusion 360, Waterjet, CNC milling, Carbon fibre layup, 3D printing, ESP32, Arduino
Outcomes
  • Locks in 4 s, against 26 s on average for the U, cable and folding locks we timed
  • Holds the 7 kN VdS 2597 lock load with a safety factor of 1.4 in FEA
  • Carbon fibre shell close to aluminium's surface hardness (115 vs 118 HRL), at about a tenth of its tensile strength
  • Judges recommended continuing it as a capstone or a commercial product
What I did
  • Designed the ratchet-and-pawl lock: waterjet-cut 6061 aluminium that grips poles up to 50 mm and stays locked if the electronics fail
  • Ran FEA on the lock at the 7 kN load from the VdS 2597 lock guideline: safety factor 1.4
  • Made the mould and laid up the six-layer carbon fibre shell, vacuum bagged
  • Debugged the electronics and integrated three controller boards into the lock case
The problem

The locks we timed took 26 s on average to lock, bent over the rack, and 70% of the 37 cyclists we surveyed go back to check their lock.

Team of 6: 2 mechanical, 1 embedded, 1 UI/UX, 1 mobile app, 1 electronics and PM

Locking a bike usually means bending over the rack and fiddling with a key, and lending it means lending the key too. For SUTD’s 30.007 Engineering Design Innovation module (theme: the travel experience), our team of six designed Click&Go: a lock on the front fork that clicks onto a pole when you push the bike in, and unlocks from an app, a key fob or a fingerprint. We aimed for a product that could stand next to commercial locks, not a class prototype.
