Part 2 of 5My work

FIRST Tech Challenge, 2013

FIRST Tech Challenge (FTC) is FIRST’s robotics competition for students aged 12 to 18, with a new game every September. The game for 2013 to 2014 was called Block Party! It was played on a 12 ft (3.7 m) square field around a central bridge with crates on pendulums. Robots scored small cubes in the crates and in goals on the floor, and in the last 30 seconds raised their alliance’s flag and hung from the bar in the middle. Each match opened with 30 seconds of autonomous play.

In 2013 I captained team 309, the Angry Melons, from the School of Science and Technology (SST). Our robot stretched up to its full height, swept cubes in with two spinning flap wheels and dropped them into the crates, raised the flag with a fast spinner, and hooked onto the bar to hang.

At FIRST Tech Challenge Singapore, at the Science Centre from 27 to 29 November 2013, we qualified 2nd and finished as tournament runner-up. We also won the Judges’ Award (Design) and the Promote Award, and SST took the Overall Best Performing School Award.

The video and the stills from it are my teammate Chi Han Looi’s.

VEX Robotics Competition, 2013 to 2015

Skyrise was the VEX Robotics Competition’s game for the 2014 to 2015 season. On a 12 ft (3.7 m) square field, two alliances of two robots score cubes of their colour on posts, in goals on the floor and on their Skyrise, a tower each alliance builds by stacking Skyrise sections on its base.

Our SST team, 8068A IsoElectronic, built its Skyrise robot as a tall lift on a low drive base, with an arm at the top that reached out over the posts. It could build our Skyrise seven sections high, and pick cubes off the floor to stack on the posts and the Skyrise. I worked on its mechanical design and build, and drove it.

At Singapore’s VEX Robotics Championship we came 2nd runner-up in Programming Skills in 2013, and in 2014 were tournament runner-up with the Robot Design Award. In December 2014, at the Asia-Pacific VEX Robotics Championship in Guangzhou, China, the Skyrise robot qualified 5th, finished 2nd runner-up overall and won the Robot Design Award. We rebuilt it for the VEX Robotics World Championship 2015 in Louisville, Kentucky, where we qualified as the 9th seed and won the Judges’ Award (Design) and the Sportsmanship Award.

Part 4 of 5My work

National Junior Robotics Competition, 2016

The National Junior Robotics Competition (NJRC) is organised by Science Centre Singapore. In my first year at Ngee Ann Polytechnic I entered its tertiary division with team YOLO. That year it ran the World Robot Olympiad’s (WRO) tasks.

The task was set in a recycling plant. Small containers of sorted waste start out on the mat, and the robot takes each to its matching recycling tank. Ours was a LEGO Mindstorms EV3 robot that follows the black lines across the mat, and it won 1st for Programming.

The video and the stills from it are my teammate Chi Han Looi’s.

Part 5 of 5My work

National Robotics Competition, 2017

In 2017 the National Junior Robotics Competition (NJRC) became the National Robotics Competition (NRC), and I entered its tertiary division again, this time with Team Atom. That division also served as Singapore’s round of the World Robot Olympiad (WRO).

Our robot was a LEGO Mindstorms EV3 again. It follows the black lines across a white mat, picks up coloured blocks and a ball, and carries them to trays. It won 1st for Mechanical Design, and our presentation came 2nd.
