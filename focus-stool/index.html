The problem

A studio stool that keeps you sitting up: more functional than comfortable, it gives a little, so your core does the work instead of a backrest.

In July 2023 I audited Digital Design and Fabrication, an architecture class at SUTD, for fun and no grade. The assignment was a stool, with a CNC-milled wooden seat on welded steel legs. I designed and built mine alone: the model and toolpaths in Fusion 360, the seat on a CNC router, the steel plates on a waterjet, and the legs welded from rebar.

I called it the Focus Stool. The rings on the seat are a target to aim for when you sit down, and the legs cross in the middle like light through the focus of a lens.

Why three slanted bars

Section titled “Why three slanted bars”

I wanted a stool that moves with you: springy, and more functional than comfortable, so you sit up and use your core instead of slumping. That’s why the legs are three straight bars of rebar that lean about 35° and cross halfway down, instead of four upright legs.

  • Upright legs are stiff. Your weight runs straight down them and squashes them along their length, which steel barely notices.
  • These legs pivot where they cross. The three bars cross close together near the middle, so the seat can tip about that crossing, like a seesaw, with almost no change in the bars’ lengths. What holds it level is the bars bending at their welds, and a bar bends far more easily than it squashes. So the seat gives a little when you shift and springs back when you sit up again: a spring with no spring in it.
  • You sit above the pivot, so sitting on it is a little like balancing: the bars push you back upright, and your core does the rest. That’s the focus.
  • Three legs never wobble. Three points always sit flat, so it stands steady on any floor however rough the build, and the only movement is the movement I designed in.
  • Straight bars are easy to make. Nothing to bend: three equal lengths cut from one 2 m bar.

The seat

Section titled “The seat”

The seat is cut on a CNC router with a 6 mm ball end mill, into a dish that curves one way. Normally you set the stepover, the distance between passes, smaller than the cutter so the passes blend into a smooth surface. Set it wider than the cutter, ignore the warnings, and each pass leaves its own groove with uncut wood between: those are the rings, a cusp turned into a feature. I tried rings and spirals in the CAM set-up, 12 to 69 mm apart; a spiral would be tedious to draw in Fusion 360, but CAM makes one easily.

I finished it in teak oil. The grooves soak up more oil than the rest and go darker, like the rings of the tree it came from.

The base: the waterjet’s idea of a circle

Section titled “The base: the waterjet’s idea of a circle”

I meant to cut a circle. Mine was the first stool in the class to have its base cut on the waterjet, on the big machine, and partway through the cut the clamp slipped. The plate drifted, and the jet cut its own idea of a circle around a perfectly round hole.

It is what it is, and we make the best of it. I kept it, trimmed it on the bandsaw and rounded its corners with a flap wheel. It’s the stool’s most expressive part.

The legs

Section titled “The legs”

Taped together at the crossing, the three bars stand up on their own like a teepee, so I could set them on the base ring and tack-weld them with a stick welder before welding on the top plate. The bars weren’t cut exactly the same length, so I welded the top plate level and left the extra rebar sticking up, then ground it off with an angle grinder and cleaned up the welds so the seat sits flush.

    Tack-welding the legs to the base ring. Taped at the crossing, the bars hold themselves up; the top plate waits on the table.

  • ⤢

    The frame welded, before grinding: the bars' ends still stand proud at the plate's edge.

Did it hold?

Section titled “Did it hold?”

It did. My instructor liked it enough to sit on it for the rest of the class while he reviewed everyone else’s projects. It also slides under the studio tables, and the slope of the back legs makes a footrest.
