The problem

A grouting robot that one person can carry and that survives grout and water on site had to move from prototype parts to small-batch production.

A five-month internship at Fabrica.AI in Singapore in 2023, as mechanical design and hardware intern, through SGInnovate’s Summation programme, which places students in deep tech startups. Fabrica.AI builds an autonomous floor-tile grouting robot that one person can carry and that has to stand up to grout and water. I maintained and changed the CAD of Version 4, the robot then in use, redesigned its sub-assemblies so they were cheaper to make and quicker to build, and took parts into small-batch production with overseas manufacturers. The updated robot went into trial runs on construction sites.

Part 2 of 2My work

Overview

In Fusion 360, I worked with the electrical and assembly teams to improve Version 4’s design and fold in their updates.

Design for manufacture and assembly

Section titled “Design for manufacture and assembly”

I simplified the robot’s sub-assemblies: less complex geometry, mating and self-locating features so parts line up as they go together, and the same fastener lengths and sizes across every BOM. The fastener count fell by 30% and assembly got simpler.

Small-batch production overseas

Section titled “Small-batch production overseas”

I worked with overseas manufacturers to begin small-batch production of the robot with new manufacturing techniques. I made the drawings and applied GD&T, which cut manufacturing cost by up to 20%.

From 3D printing to sheet metal, machining and vacuum casting

Section titled “From 3D printing to sheet metal, machining and vacuum casting”

I redesigned components that had been FDM 3D printed so they could be made from sheet metal, CNC-machined POM or vacuum cast instead, and benchmarked the parts the manufacturers sent back against our in-house standards for performance and accuracy. The new parts were smaller and stronger, and stood up better to grout and water.

A revamped electrical system in the old footprint

Section titled “A revamped electrical system in the old footprint”

I worked with the PCB designer to revamp the robot’s electrical system without growing the space it fits in, fitted the new PCB footprints, and added better access points for electrical troubleshooting.

Results

Section titled “Results”

An updated version of the robot went into trial runs on construction sites, and some of its sub-assemblies could be made overseas.
