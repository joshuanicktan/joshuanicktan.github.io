What I did
  • Developed a scalable way to make wire-electrode EHD pumps with only a laser cutter, in 20 minutes and for about S$0.80 of material each
  • Varied electrode spacing and offset in 0.05 mm steps to check the simulations and find the best layouts for pressure and for flow
  • Raised the pumps' power density by 50% with wire electrodes
  • Characterised pressure-flow (P-Q) curves across voltages and electrode configurations in LabVIEW
  • Co-authored the 2025 paper in Advanced Intelligent Systems
The problem

Soft robots need small, silent pumps. EHD pumps have no moving parts, but discharges and uneven fabrication limit their reliability and power.

A three-month research placement in 2024 in the Maeda Lab (Smart Materials and Robotics) at Tokyo Institute of Technology, now Institute of Science Tokyo. The lab works on electrohydrodynamic (EHD) pumps, which move a liquid with an electric field and have no moving parts, so they can be small and silent enough for soft robots. I developed a way to make wire-electrode pumps with nothing but a laser cutter, raised their power density by 50%, and measured how they performed.

The work fed into a paper I co-authored: A Compact, Self-Recovering Wire Electrode Electrohydrodynamic Pump for High-Speed McKibben Artificial Muscle Actuation, Advanced Intelligent Systems, 2025. The 25 × 25 × 5 mm figures are from my own prototypes; the rest of these pages comes from the paper.

Cover photo and figures: A. Marzuq, Y. Kuwajima, J. Tan et al., A Compact, Self-Recovering Wire Electrode Electrohydrodynamic Pump for High-Speed McKibben Artificial Muscle Actuation, Advanced Intelligent Systems, 2025, under CC BY 4.0. Cropped, with panel letters removed.
