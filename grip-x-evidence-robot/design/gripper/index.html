Part of Grip-X evidence-handling robot

Adaptive gripper

The gripper had to pick up objects of different shapes, sizes and surfaces from a cluttered tray, one after another. I designed it to carry objects up to 200 mm wide and 1 kg in weight.

A reverse double four-bar

Section titled “A reverse double four-bar”

The fingers sit on a reverse double four-bar (RD4B): two four-bar linkages geared to move in opposite directions, so the jaws open wide while staying parallel. A DC motor drives it, and its high torque lets the gripper hold objects up to 1.75 kg reliably, well beyond the 1 kg target.

Position, then force

Section titled “Position, then force”

As the bar linkages rotate, the centre of the pads moves up and down a little, which would throw off a grasp. So the gripper works in two steps. First a position control loop, reading the rotation sensor, opens the jaws to the width of the object. Then a pressure control mode, reading the force-sensitive resistor, squeezes just hard enough to hold it, so objects don’t slip.
