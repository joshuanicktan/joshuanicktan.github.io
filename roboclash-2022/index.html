The problem

Build a teleoperated robot that picks up and sorts small foam balls and large cubes, inside a size limit, in one month as a first-term student.

Team

Team of 4

My first robot at SUTD. In my first term I competed in RoboClash 2022 (Food Rush), the competition SUTD’s robotics club SOAR runs with LionsBot, in a team of four, and I led the design. In one month I learnt CAD, laser cutting and 3D printing, and we built a robot that picked up foam balls, sorted them and carried three cubes at once. After four losses on the first day of placement matches, we formed an alliance with team 1023MB and won the finals.

A year later I was on the other side: I led the committee that ran RoboClash 2023.

Overview

The game

Section titled “The game”

RoboClash 2022 ran from 10 to 14 January 2022 at SUTD, for teams of four SUTD students. Its game was Food Rush, themed on food delivery: on a 24 ft by 8 ft wooden playfield, teleoperated robots picked up and sorted small foam balls and large cubes. Every robot had to start inside a size limit.

Designing to the size limit

Section titled “Designing to the size limit”

I designed the robot in Fusion 360 so that its arms and intake fitted inside the size limit at the start of a match, then unfolded once it began. Modelling the whole robot first let us check the folded size before cutting anything.

Built from scrap for S$80

Section titled “Built from scrap for S$80”

Every part was laser cut or 3D printed, down to the mecanum wheels, which got heat-shrink tubing for grip. We built it from scrap acrylic sheets and aluminium extrusion, so the design had to work around the odd sizes and shapes of what was left over. The whole robot cost S$80.

Power

Section titled “Power”

The robot’s components needed different voltages and amounts of power, so I made a power distribution board on perf board to handle all of them in one place.

Competition

Section titled “Competition”

We lost all four placement matches on the first day and ended it second from the bottom. We kept showing what the robot could do, and by the finals four of the six finalists had noticed it. We formed an alliance with team 1023MB and won the finals, and the robot won the EPD Design Award: it had the fastest ball pick-up and deposit of the competition and carried three cubes at once. It won over the audience too, as the fan-favourite orange giant dinosaur.

Afterwards

Section titled “Afterwards”

The robot went on show in an acrylic case, and SOAR’s sponsorship pitch for the next competition used it as its example of the design process in Fusion 360. By then I was leading the committee that organised RoboClash 2023.
